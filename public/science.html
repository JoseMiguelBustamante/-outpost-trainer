The science behind it

Real space weather. One shared battery. A Moon mission you can replay in your browser — no download or account needed.

Where the storms come from

Our storm records come from NASA DONKI: the Space Weather Database Of Notifications, Knowledge, Information.

CME — Coronal Mass Ejection: the Sun throws a huge cloud of hot, electrically charged gas into space. DONKI records its speed in kilometers per second (km/s).

GST — Geomagnetic Storm: solar activity can shake Earth's magnetic field. The Kp index measures that disturbance on a scale from 0 to 9; bigger numbers mean stronger disturbances at Earth, not a direct measurement of danger on the Moon.

CME speedControls shield damage per second: faster means stronger. Speeds from 400–2,000 km/s map to 0.35–3.5 damage/s, clamped at those limits.
GST kpIndexControls severity using the event's peak Kp. Kp 5–9 maps to 0.35–3.5 damage/s; values below 5 are skipped.

The in-game storm banner shows the real NASA event ID, so you can connect the challenge to its source. Stronger mapped storms are shorter; mission timing and shield damage are game rules, not a space-weather forecast.

Try again with a different power plan: the seeded storm order can change between missions, while every event still has a real source.

The fallback dataset

These real event records are embedded in the game build, so the game always has real data even if the API is unreachable. Their shield damage and duration are hand-tuned for play rather than recalculated by the live-data mapping.

Why the lunar south pole

NASA's Artemis missions connect our outpost to real plans for exploring the lunar south pole. The game compresses roughly 14 Earth days of lunar night per cycle into a short challenge; actual polar sunlight depends on terrain and season. The Moon has no substantial atmosphere, so our game sends CMEs straight against a fictional shield. In reality, charged particles and radiation are hazards, but a GST describes Earth's magnetic response — it is only a difficulty proxy here.

One battery, three systems

Your shield, oxygen recycler, and communications share one power budget. Giving more power to one leaves less for the others, and solar power cannot refill the battery during the game's night. Engineers call this a tradeoff: keep the crew breathing, protect the base, and choose when to scan for resources. There is no free upgrade — try another balance on your next mission.
